Schedule & Topics

Two hands-on mornings. Each stands on its own: come to either day, or take both for more practice.

The times illustrate an 8:00 a.m.–noon format. Each work block allows about 8 minutes for an idea, 30 minutes to try it, 7 minutes to discuss what happened, and 5 minutes to set up the next step.

You can use your own permitted materials or our fictional Cedarline practice folders. The two folders are separate; get the materials before class if you like.

A course habit

Ask freely. Approve actions carefully.

Ask the tool what it can see, what it needs, and what it proposes. Review consequential changes before allowing them.

Work Beyond Chat

Block 01 · Workspace and permissions

Set up a safe workspace

Choose a project, a model, and a bounded folder in ChatGPT Work or Claude Cowork. Check privacy and permission settings, and confirm what the tool can access before it acts.

Why it matters: Your choices shape what the tool can do and what you remain responsible for approving.

Block 02 · File organization

Organize files with a plan

Practice a repeatable workflow: inspect files, ask for a proposed structure, approve selected changes, and verify the result. Work on a copy and account for every original file.

Also: Decide which work to delegate and which changes require your judgment.

Block 03 · Context and constraints

Provide useful context

Give the tool your goal, audience, constraints, relevant files, and an appropriate writing sample. Ask what it knows, what is missing, and where it may be guessing.

Block 04 · Outputs and review

Create and verify an output

Choose a useful format—such as a Word document, PDF, shared document, or small website. Check the result's facts, fit, and voice against the source material before using it.

Also: Ask well, verify carefully, and keep the final judgment yours.

Session two · working plan

Make Evidence-Based Decisions

8:00 a.m.–noon

Start with a new case, even if you missed Session One. Practice moving from a question and original evidence to a recommendation, then test whether your method works on new information. The exact pacing may change after our dry run.

8:00–8:50

Block 05 · Questions and sources

Frame a question worth checking

Turn a decision into a question you can investigate. Trace an AI claim to its original source, judge whether that source applies, and note what remains unknown.

8:50–9:00Break · 10 minutes

9:00–9:50

Block 06 · Evidence and limits

Evaluate evidence and uncertainty

Count a pattern in the feedback and keep the original response IDs behind the number. Distinguish survey results from support requests, and say what the records cannot tell you.

9:50–10:00Break · 10 minutes

10:00–10:50

Block 07 · Decision aids

Build a useful decision aid

Build a small table, spreadsheet, or local page that shows checked counts and their sources. Write a short recommendation tied to the evidence, then review the aid for errors and usability.

10:50–11:00Break · 10 minutes

11:00–11:50

Block 08 · Repeatability and boundaries

Test and refine your process

Apply the workflow to new feedback, see what breaks, and revise a step. Set rules for what AI may access, what you verify, and what you approve in future work.
