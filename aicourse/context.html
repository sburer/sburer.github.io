Context is key

Two meanings of context

Context is the information you provide. It is also the finite working space available to the tool.

01 · Information

What you bring
to the work

Goal · files · audience · constraints · examples · prior decisions

02 · Working capacity

What the tool can
keep in view

Conversation · instructions · available material · current task

Meaning one

Context as useful information

The tool does not know your work until you give it a usable version of that work.

Vague request

“Make this
better.”

No audience, material, or definition of a useful result.

A useful request

“Revise this briefing for first-year MBA students. Preserve the numbers. Lead with the recommendation.”
  • Audience
  • Constraint
  • Desired output

A reusable context pack

Give the tool
a good starting
point

Short, selected information is more useful than an indiscriminate dump of material.

  1. The jobWhat are we trying to accomplish?
  2. The materialWhich permitted files or facts matter?
  3. The boundariesWhat must be preserved, avoided, or checked?
  4. The finish lineWhat would a useful result look like?

What belongs

Select context; do not shovel it in

More material is not automatically better. Include what changes the answer; leave out what does not.

Bring

Relevant sources
Current decisions
Clear limits
A concrete audience

Leave out

Irrelevant files
Sensitive material without approval
Outdated instructions
Unnecessary background

Meaning two

Context as working capacity

Tools work with a limited amount of the conversation, instructions, and available material at a given moment.

TaskSource filesConversationRevisions…

As work becomes long or changes direction, restate what matters and focus the task again.

When the thread gets messy

Recognize the signal.
Reset the work.

Signal
The tool follows an outdated direction, misses a source, or produces generic work.
Response
Pause and write a short current-state summary.
Next task
Start focused. Reattach the essential source material and the current goal.

Try it now

Draft a context pack
for one task you brought

  1. I am working on…
  2. I need the tool to…
  3. The audience is…
  4. Use these permitted materials…
  5. Do not…
  6. A useful result would…

Context is not permission. You decide what may be shared and what belongs in the task.
